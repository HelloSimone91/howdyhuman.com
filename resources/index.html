A reading trail for values-based living

Values did not begin with a worksheet, a single school of psychology, or one part of the world. This collection follows the idea across philosophy, culture, research, and daily practice, then out into the public choices we are making together now.

Every link below is free to read or has a useful free summary. This is a starting place, not a universal canon.

01 · Long roots

Older than the word “values”

People have long asked what makes a life good, how character is formed, and what we owe one another. These readings do not tell one origin story. They open several doors into that much older conversation.

  1. Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy

    Value Theory

    Read the overview

    A map of the philosophical terrain: intrinsic and instrumental value, well-being, moral value, and the basic question of what it means to call something good.

    Why it is useful and credible: The Stanford Encyclopedia is written and reviewed by subject experts and maintained by Stanford’s Metaphysics Research Lab. Start here when you want to distinguish personal priorities from the larger philosophical study of value.

  2. Aristotle · MIT Internet Classics Archive

    Nicomachean Ethics

    Read the text

    Aristotle’s account of a good life as something practiced through action and habit, not merely known in theory. Book II is especially relevant to how repeated choices shape character.

    Why it is useful and credible: This W. D. Ross translation is hosted by MIT’s long-running classics archive. It is one influential source in the Western virtue tradition, not the origin of values thinking everywhere.

  3. Confucius · Chinese Text Project

    The Analects

    Read Chinese and English

    Short teachings about cultivating character within relationships, responsibilities, ritual, learning, and public life. It shifts attention from a private list of ideals toward the way a person becomes trustworthy with others.

    Why it is useful and credible: The Chinese Text Project is an open digital library founded by sinologist Donald Sturgeon. It places the source text beside an established James Legge translation so readers can explore context rather than rely on isolated quotations.

  4. Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy

    African Ethics

    Explore the entry

    An introduction to ethical ideas grounded in character, community, human welfare, and harmonious social life, with particular attention to Akan thought and careful limits on claims about a single “African” view.

    Why it is useful and credible: Philosopher Kwame Gyekye’s expert-reviewed entry gives readers a serious route into traditions too often left out of popular values lists. It also shows why cultural traditions should not be treated as interchangeable.

02 · Modern research

How values became something researchers could study

Twentieth- and twenty-first-century researchers turned “what matters” into models, surveys, and testable questions. The useful move is not to treat one model as the final list, but to see what each is designed to explain.

  1. Shalom H. Schwartz · Online Readings in Psychology and Culture

    An Overview of the Theory of Basic Human Values

    Read the open-access paper

    A clear explanation of the widely used circular model of human values, including why values can support one another or pull in competing directions.

    Why it is useful and credible: Schwartz developed the theory through decades of cross-cultural social-psychology research. This peer-reviewed overview is openly hosted by Grand Valley State University and explains both the model and its evidence.

  2. Schwartz and colleagues · Journal of Personality and Social Psychology

    Refining the Theory of Basic Individual Values

    Read the study record

    The 2012 refinement expands the original ten broad value types into nineteen more precise ones. It is a useful example of a theory evolving when researchers find that people make finer distinctions.

    Why it is useful and credible: The paper was peer reviewed, published in a leading psychology journal, and is indexed by the U.S. National Library of Medicine. PubMed provides the abstract and permanent DOI even where the full paper may require access.

  3. Hayes, Luoma, Bond, Masuda & Lillis · Behaviour Research and Therapy

    Acceptance and Commitment Therapy: Model, Processes and Outcomes

    Read the paper record

    A foundational account of Acceptance and Commitment Therapy, the modern clinical tradition most closely associated with values as chosen directions expressed through ongoing behavior.

    Why it is useful and credible: It was written by core ACT researchers, peer reviewed, and indexed by PubMed. The authors are also appropriately cautious: early evidence was promising, while many process questions still needed study.

  4. Reilly and colleagues · Journal of Contextual Behavioral Science

    A Systematic Review of Values Measures in ACT Research

    Read the study record

    A review of eight tools researchers use to measure values and valued action. It helps separate a useful reflection exercise from a well-supported research measure.

    Why it is useful and credible: The review examined 65 studies and reports strengths as well as gaps, including limited work with diverse populations. It is published in ACT’s specialist peer-reviewed journal and shared by its professional scientific association.

03 · From words to action

Notice the direction. Choose the next verb.

A value becomes more useful when it can guide a choice you can actually observe. These resources help with the movement from naming what matters to noticing the gap and choosing an action.

  1. RMIT University Counselling Service

    Your Values and Direction

    Work through the guide

    A short, approachable ACT learning module with audio, reflection prompts, and a worksheet for connecting values to achievements and actions.

    Why it is useful and credible: It was created by the counselling service at RMIT University in Australia. The material keeps the distinction between a continuing direction and a finishable goal clear, then invites you to apply it.

  2. Loyola University Maryland Counseling Center

    Resilient Actions

    Try the action sequence

    A practical sequence for identifying a value, choosing a goal in its service, breaking that goal into actions, and checking how your week reflected what matters.

    Why it is useful and credible: Loyola’s university counseling team places the exercise within a broader, evidence-informed resilience program. It is educational rather than diagnostic and gives the reflection a concrete next step.

  3. Howdy Human

    The Field Guide

    Browse 146 values

    A plain-language dictionary that connects each value to verbs, examples, and nearby ideas. Search for a value you already know, or begin with an action and see what direction it might express.

    Why it is useful: Howdy Human is designed as a bridge between reflection and observable action. It is an editorial tool, not a clinical assessment or a claim that one list can contain every culture’s language for what matters.

04 · Values in public life

What are we choosing together?

Values are not only personal. They shape institutions, technologies, economies, and relationships with the living world. These projects show current disagreements and experiments at a global scale.

  1. World Values Survey Association

    World Values Survey, Wave 7

    Explore the data and reports

    Survey data from more than 80,000 respondents across 64 countries and societies, covering trust, family, religion, work, politics, identity, and social change.

    Why it is useful and credible: The WVS is a long-running nonprofit network of social scientists. It publishes its questionnaires, technical documentation, datasets, and citation details, so readers can examine how broad claims about “global values” were produced.

  2. European Social Survey

    The Human Values Scale

    Read the findings guide

    An approachable guide to what a large cross-national survey found when it used Schwartz’s Portrait Values Questionnaire across Europe.

    Why it is useful and credible: The ESS is an academically led research infrastructure known for rigorous comparative methods and open documentation. The guide makes the method visible and helps readers compare patterns without mistaking country averages for every individual.

  3. IPBES · United Nations member-state platform

    Assessment of the Diverse Values and Valuation of Nature

    Explore the assessment

    A global assessment of the many ways people value nature, including living from it, living with it, living in it, and living as part of it. It asks whose values count in environmental decisions.

    Why it is useful and credible: IPBES brings scientists, Indigenous knowledge holders, and governments together to assess evidence for policy. This work explicitly challenges the habit of reducing nature’s value to a single monetary measure.

  4. UNESCO · 193 member states

    Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence

    Read the global recommendation

    A global framework connecting values such as human dignity, fairness, cultural diversity, and environmental well-being to concrete decisions about data, education, labor, health, and AI governance.

    Why it is useful and credible: Adopted by UNESCO’s member states in 2021, it shows values being negotiated across cultures and translated into public commitments. It is especially useful for seeing the hard part: moving from a principle to policy and accountability.

A small way to read a big idea

As you read, notice where a source treats values as ideals, character, motivation, relationship, direction, measurement, or public agreement. Those meanings overlap, but they are not identical.

Then bring it back to life: What direction does this make easier to name? Where is there a gap between that direction and what is happening now? What is one observable action that would move a little closer?
